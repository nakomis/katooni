# Katooni main board: design (KATOO-1)

The cable tester's only board, assembled entirely by JLCPCB. Two clusters of test ports sit along
the front edge; you plug one end of a cable into the A cluster and the other into the B cluster.
The board is generated by code (`build.py`, `board/katooni_board.py`), cloned from tinycrypt's
`hardware/key-board/` generator. The design reasons are in `docs/design.md`; this file is the
circuit.

## Circuit

### Power

The house JST-SH connector (J1), exactly as on tinycrypt: VBUS → PTC (F1) → board `VBUS`, with
the SMBJ5.0A (D1) from `VBUS` to GND, 5.1 kΩ Rd on CC (a plain 5 V sink, no PD), and the USBLC6
on D+/D− to the module's native USB (GPIO19/20). The AP2112K makes 3.3 V. The supply is 5 V at
1 A at most, shared by the board (~0.45 A peak with Wi-Fi transmitting and the buzzer on) and the
load test (~0.24 A). The firmware should not sound the buzzer during a load test.

### Test lines

Every conductor of every test port is a **line**: a series resistor to one TCA9555 pin. The
firmware drives one line low and reads every other line with the expanders' internal 100 kΩ pull-ups:
any line that reads low is connected to it through the cable.

**Why TCA9555, not MCP23017:** the first draft used MCP23017s, but on current MCP23017 datasheets
GPA7 and GPB7 are **output-only**, and every line must be read as well as driven. All sixteen
TCA9555 pins are bidirectional; it is also cheaper. Its pull-ups are always on (the TCA9535 is the
same chip without them), which is harmless here: on the CC lines they add ~30 µA through the series
resistor, far below anything that disturbs Rp/Rd detection or BMC signalling. It has no reset pin;
the firmware re-initialises the expanders over I²C.

| End | Port | Lines |
|---|---|---|
| A | USB-C (J2) | CC1, CC2, D+/D− on row A, D+/D− on row B, SBU1, SBU2, 4 SuperSpeed pairs, shell |
| A | USB-A 3.0 (J3) | D−, D+, SSRX±, GND_DRAIN, SSTX±, shell |
| A | RJ45 (J4) | pins 1–8, shield |
| A | shared | `A_VBUS`, `A_GND` (the USB-C and USB-A VBUS and GND pins) |
| B | USB-C (J5) | as J2 |
| B | micro-B (J6) | D−, D+, ID, shell |
| B | RJ45 (J7) | pins 1–8, shield |
| B | shared | `B_VBUS`, `B_GND` (the USB-C and micro-B VBUS and GND pins) |

That is 68 lines on five expanders (U10–U14 at 0x20–0x24 on I²C bus 0). A plug only touches one of
the receptacle's D+/D− pairs, so which pair a cable's D+ reaches also gives the plug orientation.

Series resistors are 1 kΩ, except the CC lines: **10 kΩ at the A end**, because the FUSB302 puts
VCONN (5 V) on one of them and 10 kΩ keeps the expander's clamp current to ~0.1 mA; **5.1 kΩ at the
B end**, so that driving a B-end CC line low presents a sink's Rd and the cable sees a partner.

The four VBUS/GND force nets carry INA226 inputs (~10 µA bias each), so they get **10 kΩ pull-ups
to 3.3 V**: the expanders' weak 100 kΩ internal pull-ups could otherwise be dragged below VIH and
read as "connected". During a load test these nets sit at up to 5 V; the 1 kΩ series resistors
limit the expander clamp current to ~1 mA, and the firmware keeps those pins as inputs.

### E-marker (FUSB302B, U4)

On the A-end USB-C's CC1/CC2, on I²C bus 1 (its address, 0x22, is inside the expanders' range,
hence a second bus). VCONN comes from `VBUS` through its internal switch to whichever CC pin is
the cable's VCONN pin. The firmware must leave the CC lines' expander pins as inputs during PD
traffic, and switch the FUSB302's CC pull-ups/pull-downs off during a continuity scan.

### Resistance check (Kelvin)

The test current runs `VBUS` → Q1 (AO3401A, switched by Q2 from `LOAD_EN`) → 20 Ω → 100 mΩ shunt →
`A_VBUS` → **cable VBUS** → `B_VBUS` → Q4/Q5 (back-to-back AO3400A, `BSHORT_EN`) → `B_GND` →
**cable GND** → `A_GND` → Q3 (AO3400A, `AGND_EN`) → GND. About 0.24 A.

- **U5** (INA226, 0x40) measures the current across the shunt.
- **U6** (0x41) measures `A_VBUS_S − B_VBUS_S`: the drop along the cable's VBUS conductor.
- **U7** (0x44) measures `B_GND_S − A_GND_S`: the drop along its GND conductor.

**Kelvin through the plug's own contacts.** On each USB-C receptacle one pin per rail carries the
current (A4 VBUS, A1 GND) and another senses (A9 VBUS, A12 GND). They touch separate plug contacts,
which the plug joins inside itself, so the sense pins read the voltage at the plug with no current
in them. The B-row VBUS/GND pins (B1, B4, B9, B12) are deliberately unconnected: the B row's pads can
only leave forward under the receptacle body, and with all of them in use the router always stranded
one. 0.24 A is far inside one contact's rating. With no USB-C plug in (USB-A or micro-B cables), a 10 Ω resistor
from each sense net to its force net makes the sense follow the force; at 10 µA of INA226 bias
that's 0.1 mV of error, and with a USB-C plug in, the 10 Ω carries a negligible share.

At 0.24 A the INA226's ±81.92 mV range covers up to ~340 mΩ per conductor (a 1.5 m 28 AWG
conductor is ~320 mΩ), at 2.5 µV / 0.24 A ≈ 10 µΩ resolution. 0.24 A is Claude's call (the sketch
said 0.5–1 A): more current would only shrink that range, and it keeps the 1 A budget easy.

**Why back-to-back at the B end:** a single MOSFET's body diode would join `B_VBUS` and `B_GND`
whenever the firmware drives one low in a continuity scan. Two in series, sources joined, block both
ways when off.

**Pulse, don't hold.** The 20 Ω dissipates ~1.2 W while on, so the firmware switches the load for
tens of milliseconds per measurement. The 2512 is rated 1 W continuous.

### Display

HS20HS072RX: 2.0" 240×320 IPS, ST7789, 4-wire SPI, 12-pin 0.5 mm FPC: 1 GND, 2 CS, 3 RS (D/C),
4 SCL, 5 SDA, 6 RST, 7 NC, 8 IOVCC, 9 VCC, 10 A, 11 K, 12 GND. It clips into J8, a dual-contact ZIF,
so the tail can go in either face up. The backlight runs from `VBUS` through 100 Ω (~19 mA at a
~3.1 V forward voltage) and Q6 on its cathode (`LCD_BL`, PWM-able).

### Buzzer

MLT-8530 from 3.3 V, switched low-side by Q7 (`BUZZER`, PWM at ~2.7 kHz), with a 1N4148WS
flyback diode.

## Board

- 100 × 70 mm, **4 layers**: F.Cu signals, In1.Cu a solid GND plane, In2.Cu and B.Cu signals,
  with GND pours on F.Cu and B.Cu. 1.6 mm, ENIG, black mask, white silk (as tinycrypt).
- **GND is never autorouted.** Every SMD GND pad gets a short fanout track to its own via into the
  plane before routing (`board/gnd.py`); after routing, stitching vias on a 2.5 mm grid tie the
  outer pours to the plane. Freerouting left about a third of the GND connections open when it had to
  find them itself.
- **The analog block** (FUSB302, INA226s, load switches, shunt) sits in the band between the module
  and the expanders, not by the ports, so the 68 test lines have the space between the resistor rows
  and the ports to themselves.
- **Front edge (y = 70):** the test ports, openings flush with the edge. A cluster on the left:
  RJ45, USB-A, USB-C; B cluster on the right: USB-C, micro-B, RJ45.
- **Back edge (y = 0):** the module, its antenna at the edge with a copper keep-out (all layers)
  under and beside it; the house JST connector on the left, opening backwards; the display ZIF.
- Four M2 holes.

## Ordering notes (JLCPCB)

- **Through-hole parts** (RJ45 × 2, USB-A, and the USB-C and micro-B shell pegs) mean JLCPCB's
  **Standard** PCBA rather than Economic, which costs more to set up. Check at order time.
- About 17 extended parts: ~$51 of loading fees per order.
- Buy the **HS20HS072RX panels** (C5329582) in the same LCSC order; JLCPCB doesn't fit them.
- **Check before ordering:** the panel's datasheet pin 1 matches J8 pin 1 with the tail in the
  orientation the case gives it; the rotations of the connectors in JLCPCB's placement preview;
  that the USB-C receptacles are the 24-pin CX90B1-24P (not a 16-pin lookalike).
- **No reverse-polarity protection beyond the TVS and PTC:** check the house cable's pinout with a
  meter before the first plug-in, as for tinycrypt.

## Not in this board

- No battery, no USB-PD source (VBUS is the house 5 V).
- No ESD protection on the test ports beyond the series resistors.
- No HDMI, Lightning or other connectors.
